# Contributor management: behaviour rules

_What the platform does today for users, organisations and virtual contributors as contributors: how they come into being, their profiles and settings, who sees their personal data, organisation verification, model cards, renaming and deletion: what it does, why, who may, and what is still to answer. Membership of Spaces and organisations, invitations, licensing and notifications have their own files. The layout is the one piloted in `communications.md`: **1. Rules**, **2. Rights**, **3. Open questions**, with the evidence in an **Appendix**._

_Open questions per owner: Product Lead 15 · Technical Lead 4 · Quality Lead 6 · Security Lead 2 · Incident Lead 2 · Architecture Lead 1. By state: 25 open · 5 with a suggested answer. Proof of the 54 rules: 34 proven by a test · 20 untested (Appendix A)._

**How to read it**

- **Rule** is one or two plain sentences. The exact behaviour, with error messages and limits, is in Appendix A under the same id. The new core is checked against the exact text.
- **Why: business** is what Alkemio needs. **Why: product** is the user story. **Why: technical** is the engineering reason, when there is one. "—" means no source states one. The product review has not been through this file yet; it fills the business why and the user story where they are missing.
- **Decided by** names the source of the decision: a spec with its requirement, a server pull request or commit, an issue or a document. "Not decided: exists in the code" means the behaviour is there and nobody chose it. "Not decided: a defect" means today's behaviour is a fault.
- A marker in bold at the end of a rule points at section 3: **(open: F6)** means a question on this rule is still open, **(defect: F10)** means today's behaviour is a fault and must not be copied as intended, **(defect in part: F3)** means part of it is. A rule with no marker stands as written. Every rule is logged as the server behaves today, also when that is not the intended end state. Whether a test proves the rule sits in Appendix A as **proven** or **untested**.
- Hard rules are not marked yet. Some rules are choices that a new feature may challenge, and some can never change; a later version will mark the second kind with "(hard rule)" after the why that makes it so. Until then, no marker does not mean a rule is open to change.

### C. Findings and questions in full

_The original text of every finding (F) and open question (OQ), with its evidence and its ask. The text keeps the old spelling of the ids ("F-3" is F3). F1 and F2 each appear twice in section 3 (a and b) because they hold two questions for two owners._

#### Findings

- **F1 — An organisation is born without an owner (CM-33, register E2).** Code: `createOrganization` assigns the creator ASSOCIATE + ADMIN, never OWNER (`organization.service.ts:204-221`); the role definition says owners 1..3 (`organization.role.definitions.ts:62-63`) and the floor is checked only on removal. Test: `organization-settings` pins three associates (creator, Admin, Associate) with no owner. Spec: 027 A6 names Support as creator; 054 US4 calls "an organisation always has an owner" an invariant the platform declares. ACC: 126 organisations with zero owners, 40 with neither owner nor admin, 11 of 23 organisations created in 2026 with no roles at all. Ruling: who owns a Support-created organisation, and must creation satisfy the floor?
- **F2 — Admin / owner without the associate entry role (CM-34, register A6).** Code: `requiresEntryRole: true` on ADMIN and OWNER is read only by a display field (`role.set.resolver.fields.ts:245`); no service enforces it. Test: `organization-owner` › `should create organization owner` assigns OWNER to a user who is not an associate and passes — enshrining the gap. Spec 062 FR-019/US5-AS2 already plans around "an admin or owner who is not an associate". ACC: 757 credentials, 26 users. Ruling: enforce (auto-grant the entry role, or refuse), or drop the flag.
- **F3 — E-mail verification is never checked on the live registration path (CM-01, register I4).** Code: `identity-resolve.service.ts:98-108` hard-codes `emailVerified: true` ("FIXME: temporary ugly workaround"), so `registerNewUser`'s "not verified" refusal (`registration.service.ts:74-79`) is unreachable. Test: none. Spec: 026-identity-consolidation assumes verified sign-ups; nothing states that unverified identities may hold profiles. ACC: 56 users whose identity e-mail is unverified. Ruling: defect, or an accepted consequence of the OIDC flow verifying upstream?
- **F4 — Duplicate-email check is case-sensitive and runs before normalisation (CM-04).** Code: `validateUserProfileCreationRequest` looks the raw address up exactly (`user.service.ts:590-597`, `user.lookup.service.ts:69-80`) and lowercases afterwards (599); `Foo@x.io` against a stored `foo@x.io` passes the check and then fails on the `user.email` unique index with a raw database error. Test: the "twice" case hits the nameID check first. Ruling: normalise before the check (and rebaseline the message).

### F. Where this lands in the new core

Users, organisations and virtual contributors all become `actor` rows (kind user / organisation / vc, with the assistant as a fourth kind) — the catalogue's ID-3, MB-7 and AI-1 rows — read through `viewer` for the self case and `node(id)` for everyone else; profile fields, references and tagsets become vocabulary + label rows, preferences become `user_settings` attribute rows (CM-19..22 keep their semantics but the per-row merge and the `@AfterLoad` backstop become a default on the attribute), credentials attach to the actor directly as they already do (CM-54), and stored authorization policies disappear, so every "re-apply authorization after a write" step (CM-19, CM-38, CM-48) goes away and the visibility rules of CM-11..15, CM-37, CM-46/47 and CM-51 must be stated as read-time predicates on the actor — which is also where findings F-6 and F-7 (ungated root queries) must not be carried over. Kratos and the BFF session stay (ID-1/ID-2), so CM-01, CM-24, CM-28 and CM-30/31 are unchanged behaviours against an unchanged provider; account deletion and the admin identity operations move to the ops namespace (ID-4, slices 10–12) with the audit rows kept. Organisation verification becomes a `stage` on the organisation actor (CM-42); the model card (CM-52) is a property of the `ai_engine` registry rather than a resolver constant. Three facts found here should shape the model: (1) an organisation must be born with an owner (F-1) and the entry-role rule must be a constraint, not a flag (F-2); (2) e-mail verification and the duplicate-email check must be enforced at the actor boundary (F-3, F-4); (3) rename is one door (F-5).
