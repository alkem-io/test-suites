# Admin operations: behaviour rules

_What the platform does today for the work of its operators: resetting stored access rules, the tasks that track long jobs, the operational actions of the Platform Operations Admin, the scheduled background jobs, the audit trail and who reads it, and the lists of the admin console: what it does, why, who may, and what is still to answer. It follows the three-section layout accepted in the product review of `communications.md` (alkem-io/test-suites#717): **1. Rules**, **2. Rights**, **3. Open questions**, with the evidence in an **Appendix**. Product reviewed section 3 on 2026-11-20._

_Open questions per owner: Product Lead 0 · Technical Lead 11 · Quality Lead 3 · Security Lead 4 · Service Operations Lead 2. By state: 15 open · 5 with a suggested answer · 4 decided. Proof of the 45 rules: 11 proven by a test · 34 untested (Appendix A)._

_Whys of the 45 rules: 33 rest on a source or a product decision · 6 carry a technical why that is still a QA draft, for the Technical Lead (OPS-04, OPS-07, OPS-08, OPS-17, OPS-26, OPS-32) · 6 have none, and each carries an open question or a defect marker (OPS-12, OPS-18, OPS-28, OPS-35, OPS-36, OPS-48)._

**How to read it**

- **Rule** is one or two plain sentences. The exact behaviour, with error messages and limits, is in Appendix A under the same id. The new core is checked against the exact text.
- **Why: business** is what Alkemio needs. **Why: product** is the user story. **Why: technical** is the engineering reason. A rule needs at least one why, and gets a second or third only where it adds something for the decision or the code. "—" means none is given.
- A why that names a spec, issue, pull request, code comment or code line rests on that source. A why, or the part of a why, ending in _(draft)_ was written by QA and no source states it: product keeps, rewrites or deletes it, and sets **Decided by**. A user story always ends in its "so that"; where no source gives one, QA wrote it. A rule with no why at all carries an open question or a defect marker in section 3.
- **Decided by** names the source of the decision: a spec, a commit, a pull request, an issue or a document. "Not decided: exists in the code" means the behaviour is in the code and nobody chose it. "Not decided: a defect" means today's behaviour is a fault.
- A marker in bold at the end of a rule points at section 3: **(open: F3)** means a question on this rule is still open, **(defect: F2)** means today's behaviour is a fault and must not be copied as intended, **(defect in part: F5)** means part of it is. A rule with no marker stands as written. Every rule is logged as the server behaves today, also when that is not the intended end state. Whether a test proves the rule sits in Appendix A as **proven** or **untested**.
- Hard rules are not marked yet. Some rules are choices that a new feature may challenge, and some can never change; a later version will mark the second kind with "(hard rule)" after the why that makes it so. Until then, no marker does not mean a rule is open to change.

### C. Findings and questions in full

_The original text of every finding (F) and open question (OQ), with its evidence and its proposed ruling. F11 appears twice in section 3 (a and b) because it holds two questions for two owners._

#### Findings

- **F1 — The reset worker's build is unchecked (OPS-12).** Code: the worker is the same image and applies whatever rules it was built with; no version or schema check. Test: `destructive-positives` skips the reset-all positive for this reason; the client checklist records the 2026-09-21 incident (worker image from 2026-08-10 rewrote every policy with pre-027 rules). Spec: 032 says a reset "must not alter the role's own grant set" (idempotent) — true only when the images match. Infra: two separate pins with a comment. Ruling: add a guard (worker refuses or warns when its version differs from the API's, or the publisher stamps the expected version into each message), or make the deploy coupling mechanical; until then record it as an operations rule.
- **F2 — A skipped subtree is a silent success (OPS-05).** Code: `resilientCascade` logs and returns `[]`; the task records the entity as finished. Test: none. Data: crosscheck F2 (retired-credential rules on 1,018 files, 872 buckets, 2,000 tagsets … under fresh parents) and F3 (empty-rule policies that deny everyone) are exactly this pattern on ACC. Ruling: the task should report skipped steps (or the row should be repaired), and the register should split "orphan" from "live but skipped".
- **F3 — Orphan policies have no sweep (OPS-36).** Code: reset-all walks live records only (#6614); nothing scheduled touches `authorization_policy`. Test: the orphan plan says the "every remaining policy is reachable" property "has no covering test and cannot have one here". Data: G1 ~9,400 orphan rows; #6614 counted 13,928 retired-credential policies on an ACC copy; #6657/#6655 is a one-off reversible migration. Ruling: one-off only (accepted until the new core) or a recurring sweep?
- **F4 — Operations rows always say `platform_admin` (OPS-41).** Code: `platform.operations.audit.service.ts:96` writes the coarse tier for every operations row, "by design". Spec: 027 FR-025 reserves `platform_admin` for an action a legacy broad grant authorized during Slice A and makes it unwritable after Slice B; "no record may imply a broader role than the one that authorized it". Test: none asserts an operations row's initiator role. Ruling: attribute operations rows through `resolveInitiatorRole` like the other families, or amend FR-025 to name the operations family as a permanent carve-out.
- **F5 — Task queries are open to everyone (OPS-18).** Code: `task` and `tasks` declare no privilege; the guard admits anonymous. Result lines name account/organisation/user ids. Test: `forum-hierarchy-reconcile` polls as the harness admin only. Spec: 006 (missing) made the REST route "internal by construction"; nothing says the GraphQL twin is public. Ruling: gate on the operational privilege (or on the task's creator) and strip ids from result lines (repo rule: no dynamic data in messages).
- **F6 — "Global admins access" text is stale (OPS-06).** Code: the single-policy reset grants the operations admin and content full access. Schema: "Reset the specified Authorization Policy to global admin privileges". Test: `a3.ts:110-113` comment "nobody but a legacy admin holds DELETE". Ruling: rename/redescribe the mutation (BREAKING-approved or deprecation) and fix the comment; rebaseline, not a behaviour change.
- **F7 — A refused ingest still mints a task and an audit row (OPS-28).** Code: the task is created before the gate; refusal marks it errored and writes a failure row. Test: the 13 A11 negatives assert the refusal only. Ruling: move the gate above the task creation (also removes an unauthenticated-registered path to fill Redis and the audit table), or accept and document.
- **F8 — Scheduled jobs are unauthenticated and unaudited (OPS-22).** Code: the REST routes have no guard and write no audit row (no actor). Spec: 032 FR-016 "every operational action … regardless of which role authorized it"; the REST spec (006) is missing locally. Infra: prune daily, ingest hourly use this path on acceptance and production. Ruling: give the cron a service identity (so a `system`/`service` row can be written and the route can be gated), or record the exception in FR-016.
- **F9 — Avatar migration row has no subject (OPS-48).** Code: `targetUserId` omitted although the writer's own comment names the avatar migration as the per-user example. Test: `A11.adminUpdateContributorAvatars` asserts the returned profile only. Ruling: pass the profile's owner as the subject (a profile may belong to an organisation — then `subjectOrganizationId`).

### D. Test gaps

_For the Quality Lead._

Untested rules by id: OPS-01, OPS-02, OPS-04, OPS-07, OPS-08, OPS-09, OPS-13, OPS-14, OPS-16, OPS-17, OPS-21, OPS-23, OPS-25, OPS-26, OPS-30, OPS-31, OPS-32, OPS-33, OPS-34, OPS-42, OPS-43, OPS-47, OPS-50; plus the untested clauses of OPS-11 (all-accounts loop), OPS-24 (stored copy; swallowed failure), OPS-27 (deletion effect) and OPS-40 (never deleted; survives user deletion).

Behaviours the area has that neither reading nor tests pinned down:
- The effect of any reset on a stored policy — that a rule introduced by a new build appears on an existing entity only after its reset, and that the policy of an untouched entity is byte-identical after a second reset (S-06 covers one organisation's two Support rules only).
- Worker retry and dead-letter: a reset that fails five times is dropped and the task shows the error; a reset that fails once is retried and succeeds.
- Drain: a rollout during a reset loses no message.
- Task settling: a counted task reaches COMPLETED exactly when the last item lands; duplicate delivery does not over-count; an expired task is a clean failure for pollers.
- Prune: a notification older than the retention is gone and a newer one stays; the 101st notification of a user is removed oldest-first; read state is ignored; an invalid setting deletes nothing.
- Search ingest: the alias points at the new indices and the old ones are deleted; a failure leaves the previous index serving.
- Geolocation: a location with a country and no coordinates gets them; one bad location aborts the rest.
- Avatar migration: an external avatar becomes a document in the profile bucket; an unreachable one is left alone.
- The four sweeps (drafts, signing attempts, push subscriptions, digests) firing at all, and never firing on the worker.
- Operations audit rows: one per execution, success and failure, subject NULL for platform-wide, actor named, error text capped.
- Boot-time platform reset on an empty policy.
- The REST routes: reachable in-cluster, unreachable publicly, task 404 after expiry.
- Any operation (ingest, prune, avatars, geolocation, collection cleanup, BoK refresh) on an acceptance-size dataset — duration, and for `resetLicenseOnAccounts` whether one request survives it.

### F. Where this lands in the new core

The rebuild briefing (plan § 1) removes stored authorization policies and "every reset mutation": authorization is computed at read time from credentials, so OPS-01…OPS-13 and OPS-36 are **dead-by-design** — there is nothing to reset, no worker, no version coupling, no orphan policies (register G1 and crosscheck F2/F3 become ETL concerns, not runtime ones). What survives as behaviour: the ops seat and its bounded family (OPS-10/OPS-20, "ops namespace" in the catalogue), the audit trail (OPS-40–48 — the catalogue keeps a role-attributed, append-only record; the new core should decide whether operations rows keep a coarse tier, F-4), the background jobs (drafts, signing, push, digest — OPS-30–34, each belonging to its own area's slice) and the admin console reads (OPS-50, MB-8). Three facts found here should shape the model: (1) the task queries and the internal REST surface are unauthenticated today (OPS-18, OPS-22) — the new surface must gate job status and give scheduled jobs an identity that the audit trail can name; (2) search ingest, prune and geolocation are the only operations whose *effect* matters once resets are gone, and none has an effect-level case (gaps); (3) the audit reader has no screen and cannot reach organisation-subject rows (F-11), so the audit read surface is a design input, not a port.
