# Space lifecycle: behaviour rules

_What the platform does today for the life of a Space: the top-level Space (L0), its Subspaces (L1) and sub-subspaces (L2), their URL names, creation, settings, visibility, the three conversions inside one top-level Space, the three moves to another top-level Space ("cross-L0"), deletion and reading. Membership, licensing, callouts and notifications have their own files and appear here only where a lifecycle action changes them. The file follows the three-section layout: **1. Rules**, **2. Rights**, **3. Open questions**, with the evidence in an **Appendix**._

_Open questions per owner: Product Lead 8 · Technical Lead 4 · Quality Lead 5 · Security Lead 3 · Incident Lead 1. By state: 17 open · 4 with a suggested answer. Proof of the 64 rules: 48 proven by a test · 16 untested (Appendix A)._

_Whys of the 64 rules: 46 rest on a source · 15 carry a draft for product to decide · 3 have none, and each already carries an open question or defect marker (JRN-10, JRN-42, JRN-43)._

**How to read it**

- **Rule** is one or two plain sentences. The exact behaviour, with error messages and limits, is in Appendix A under the same id. The new core is checked against the exact text.
- **Why: business** is what Alkemio needs. **Why: product** is the user story. **Why: technical** is the engineering reason. A rule needs at least one why, and gets a second or third only where it adds something for the decision or the code. "—" means none is given.
- A why that names a spec, issue, pull request, code comment or code line rests on that source. A why ending in _(draft)_ was written by QA and no source states it: product keeps, rewrites or deletes it, and sets **Decided by**. A rule with no why at all carries an open question in section 3.
- **Decided by** names the source of the decision: a spec, a commit or pull request, or an issue. "Not decided: exists in the code" means the behaviour exists in the code and nobody chose it. "Not decided: a defect" means today's behaviour is a fault. Product has not reviewed this file yet.
- A marker in bold at the end of a rule points at section 3: **(open: F1)** means a question on this rule is still open, **(defect: F6)** means today's behaviour is a fault and must not be copied as intended, **(defect in part: F3)** means part of it is. A rule with no marker stands as written. Every rule is logged as the server behaves today, also when that is not the intended end state. Whether a test proves the rule sits in Appendix A as **proven** or **untested**.
- Hard rules are not marked yet. Some rules are choices that a new feature may challenge, and some can never change; a later version will mark the second kind with "(hard rule)" after the why that makes it so. Until then, no marker does not mean a rule is open to change.
- Words: a **top-level Space** is level L0, a **Subspace** L1, a **sub-subspace** L2. A **conversion** changes the level of a Space inside its own top-level Space ("same-L0"). A **move** takes it to another top-level Space ("cross-L0"). The **URL name** is the nameID.

### C. Findings and questions in full

_The original text of every finding (F) and open question (OQ), with its evidence. Inside these texts a finding is written "F-3" or "finding 3"; section 3 calls it F3._

#### Findings

- **F1 — Updates room after a cross-L0 move (JRN-53).** Spec 084 FR-014/015 and SC-003: the announcement room is recreated **empty**; 030 FR-009 makes the UI warn "announcement channel reset". Code: `recreateUpdatesRooms` deletes and recreates it, fire-and-forget after the mutation returns. Tests: four cases named "updates room messages are preserved" pin `toHaveLength(1)` and the `move-L2-to-L1-rooms` header cites epic alkemio#1846 "Updates go with the space". They are green either because the read lands before the async recreate or because the recreate fails silently. Ruling: which is the product rule — preserved or reset? Then fix the losing side (spec+code or the four tests).
- **F2 — Licence after a move (JRN-48).** Code issues the account-type default plans to the moved space's own actor and never recomputes its licence; spec 083 FR-021b asks for explicit propagation; the three "license subscriptions inherited from target L0" cases pass only while the target holds exactly the default plans (a target with Plus would fail). Ruling: define what a moved subspace's licence is (derived from the new L0, per ENT-26) and rebaseline the oracle.
- **F3 — No level guard on conversions or creation (JRN-14, JRN-43).** The three same-L0 conversions accept any space (an L0 given to L1→L0 is renamed with a `-1` suffix; an L0 given to L2→L1 is re-parented under itself); `createSubspace` computes `level = parent + 1` without a cap and the matrix shows `space-admin@L2` holding `create-subspace`; L1→L2 does not check that the source is childless. The moves do all of this. Ruling: file as a defect (hard preconditions on all six, and refuse creation below L2).
- **F4 — Same-L0 conversions do not recompute platform access (JRN-42).** L2→L1 and L1→L2 re-apply authorization from the new parent but keep the stored `platformRolesAccess`; when the new chain differs in privacy this is the #6303 leak class (fixed only for L1→L0 and the moves). No case probes anonymous access after a same-L0 conversion. Ruling: defect.
- **F5 — Atomicity of the L1 moves (JRN-54).** 083 FR-010 "MUST be atomic — single database transaction"; code: role wipe, pending drop, structural writes and plan issue run as separate statements; 030 D-2 records the non-retrofit as risk R-3. Ruling: accept R-3 formally (amend 083) or schedule the retrofit.
- **F6 — Space creation not atomic (JRN-11; server#6648 OPEN).** Community/Matrix outside the transaction, policy applied in a later step; half-created spaces blank list pages and cannot be deleted or repaired. Known defect; no new issue.

### F. Where this lands in the new core

The space document and its tree are slice 4–5 of the rebuild. Everything above stays a behaviour; what should change in the model is what the findings expose: (1) `nameID` uniqueness becomes a database constraint scoped `(levelZeroSpaceID, nameID)` plus a global one for roots (F-7), with the restricted-word list a seed table; (2) nesting depth becomes a constraint on the parent link, not a check in six resolvers (F-3, JRN-14); (3) platform-level access (JRN-30, JRN-49) is derived at read time from the parent chain instead of a persisted record that every move and conversion must remember to recompute (F-4 is the proof that one path forgot); (4) the six conversion/move operations collapse into one "re-parent" command whose semantics follow the one axis the sources agree on — same-L0 keeps the community, cross-L0 wipes it (JRN-40/41 vs JRN-45) — executed in one transaction with the room and invitation side effects as an outbox (F-1, F-5, F-6); (5) licence derivation from the L0 (ENT-26) makes JRN-48 moot. Error texts and the restricted list are rebaseline rows, not rules.
